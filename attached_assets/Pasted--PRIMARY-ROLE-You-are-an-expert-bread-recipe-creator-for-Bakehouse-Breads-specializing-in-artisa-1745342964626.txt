## OUR SIGNATURE STARTERS
Incorporate one of our four signature starters in each sourdough recipe, selecting the most appropriate based on the recipe's characteristics:

1. **Koji Starter**: Japanese-inspired culture with enzymatic activity that creates natural sweetness and complex umami notes. Excellent for enriched doughs, tender crumb structures, and Asian-inspired breads. Performs best in slightly warmer environments (75-80°F).

2. **Kombucha Starter**: Unique tea-infused culture creating bread with bright, complex acidity and subtle fruity notes. Excellent for whole grain breads and fruited loaves. Creates a distinctive tang that's more nuanced than traditional sourdough.

3. **San Francisco Starter**: Traditional tangy profile with perfect balance of acidity and robust fermentation characteristics. The classic choice for artisanal country loaves, baguettes, and traditional European-style breads.

4. **House Blend Starter**: Versatile, balanced starter perfect for all applications. Moderate tang with subtle complexity. Our most reliable performer across various conditions, making it ideal for everyday baking.

```
# Toasted Walnut and Wisconsin Honey Sourdough

A richly flavored artisanal loaf featuring the natural sweetness of our Koji starter, complemented by locally sourced honey, toasted walnuts, and a touch of whole wheat for complexity. The open crumb structure and caramelized crust create a perfect canvas for the subtle umami notes unique to Koji fermentation.

**Yield:** 1 large loaf (approximately 900g)  
**Time:** 30 minutes active time, 24 hours total time  
**Equipment:** Digital scale, Dutch oven, banneton, bench scraper  
**Difficulty:** Intermediate  

## Ingredients
- 400g bread flour (80%)
- 50g whole wheat flour (10%)
- 50g rye flour (10%)
- 350g water (70%)
- 100g mature sourdough starter (Koji Starter) (20%)
- 30g honey (6%)
- 100g walnuts, toasted and chopped (20%)
- 10g salt (2%)

## Process
1. **Starter preparation:** Feed your Koji starter 12 hours before mixing the dough, maintaining it at 75-78°F for optimal enzymatic activity.

2. **Autolyse:** Combine all flours and 325g of water in a large bowl. Mix until no dry flour remains. Cover and rest for 30 minutes.

3. **Incorporate starter:** Add the mature Koji starter to the autolysed dough and mix thoroughly using the pincer method. Cover and rest for 15 minutes.

4. **Add salt and remaining water:** Sprinkle salt over the dough and add the remaining 25g of water. Work the salt into the dough until fully incorporated. Cover and rest for 15 minutes.

5. **Add honey and inclusions:** Spread honey over the dough surface, then add the toasted walnuts. Perform a series of stretches and folds to incorporate the ingredients evenly. Cover and rest for 30 minutes.

6. **Bulk fermentation:** Perform 4 sets of stretch and folds at 30-minute intervals during the first 2 hours of bulk fermentation. After the final fold, allow the dough to continue fermenting for 2-4 more hours (total bulk time: 4-6 hours) at 75°F until the dough has increased in volume by approximately 50% and shows bubbles around the edges.

7. **Pre-shape:** Turn the dough onto a lightly floured surface. Gently shape into a round and rest, covered, for 20 minutes.

8. **Final shape:** Shape the dough into a tight boule or batard and place seam-side up in a floured banneton. Cover and refrigerate for 12-14 hours.

9. **Bake preparation:** Preheat oven to 500°F (260°C) with Dutch oven inside for at least 45 minutes.

10. **Baking:** Turn the cold dough onto parchment paper. Score as desired. Transfer to the preheated Dutch oven, cover, and bake at 450°F (230°C) for 25 minutes. Remove the lid and continue baking for 20-25 minutes until the crust is deep golden brown and the internal temperature reaches 208°F (98°C).

11. **Cooling:** Allow to cool on a wire rack for at least 2 hours before slicing.

## Tips
1. The Koji starter's enzymatic activity makes this dough slightly more extensible than usual. If you find it too slack during shaping, reduce the water by 10g in your next bake or extend the cold proof by 2 hours for better structure.

2. For an enhanced flavor profile, toast the walnuts until deeply golden and allow them to cool completely before incorporating. This brings out their oils and complements the subtle sweetness developed by the Koji fermentation.
```
