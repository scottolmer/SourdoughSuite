# Starter School: Caring for Your Bakehouse Breads Starter

Welcome to the Bakehouse Breads family! Your new sourdough starter is a living culture that, with proper care, will provide you with delicious bread for years to come. This guide will walk you through everything you need to know about maintaining your starter's health and vitality.

## Understanding Your Starter

Your starter is a symbiotic culture of wild yeasts and beneficial bacteria. These microorganisms ferment flour and water, creating the bubbles, flavor, and leavening power that make sourdough special. Each of our specialty starters has unique characteristics that influence flavor, fermentation speed, and ideal baking applications.

## Basic Maintenance Routine

### Regular Feeding Schedule

- **Active Maintenance (Room Temperature)**: Feed once daily for regular use
- **Reduced Maintenance (Refrigerated)**: Feed once weekly for occasional use

### Standard Feeding Method

1. **Discard**: Remove all but 50g of your starter
2. **Feed**: Add 50g flour and 50g water (1:1:1 ratio)
3. **Mix**: Stir thoroughly until no dry flour remains
4. **Rest**: Cover loosely and let ferment at room temperature

### Signs of a Healthy Starter

- Doubles in volume within 4-8 hours after feeding
- Develops a pleasant, tangy aroma
- Shows consistent bubbles throughout
- Has a slightly domed surface when ready
- Passes the "float test" (a small piece floats in water)

## Specialty Starter Considerations

### Koji Starter
- Prefers slightly warmer temperatures (75-80°F)
- Benefits from 10% whole wheat flour in feeding mix
- Expect a subtle sweet aroma with complex umami notes
- May develop more liquid than other starters (this is normal)

### San Francisco Style Starter
- Performs well at standard room temperature (65-72°F)
- Develops classic tangy flavor with longer fermentation
- Can be refrigerated for up to 2 weeks between feedings
- Perfect for traditional crusty loaves with open crumb

### Kombucha Starter
- Feed every 12 hours for best results
- Benefits from occasional green tea addition (10% of water weight)
- Creates breads with subtle fruity notes and bright acidity
- Excellent for whole grain and fruit-enriched breads

### Extra Sour Starter
- Benefits from cooler fermentation (62-68°F)
- For maximum sourness, use 20-30% rye in feeding mix
- Allow to fully mature between feedings (12-24 hours)
- Creates intensely tangy breads with complex flavor

### House Blend Starter
- Most forgiving and versatile of all starters
- Adaptable to most kitchen temperatures (65-78°F)
- Balanced flavor profile suitable for any bread style
- Ideal for beginners or bakers seeking consistency

## Troubleshooting Common Issues

### Low Activity
- **Signs**: Minimal bubbling, slow rise, weak aroma
- **Solutions**: 
  - Increase feeding frequency to twice daily
  - Check water quality (use filtered if possible)
  - Ensure warmer environment (75-78°F)
  - Add 10% whole grain flour to feeding mix

### Too Much Liquid (Hooch)
- **Signs**: Gray or clear liquid on top, stronger smell
- **Solutions**:
  - Feed more frequently
  - Stir hooch back in or pour off (your preference)
  - Decrease water slightly in next feeding
  - Return to regular feeding schedule

### Mold or Off Odors
- **Signs**: Pink/orange coloration, fuzzy growth, nail polish smell
- **Solutions**: 
  - If small spot, salvage a clean portion from the bottom
  - Transfer to a clean container with fresh flour/water
  - If extensive, you may need a replacement starter
  - Ensure all tools and containers are clean

## Long-Term Storage Options

### Refrigeration (1-2 Weeks)
1. Feed your starter and let it begin to rise
2. Place in refrigerator in a container with loose-fitting lid
3. Return to room temperature and feed twice before baking

### Extended Storage (2+ Weeks)
1. Feed starter at 1:2:2 ratio (starter:flour:water)
2. Allow to ferment until just peaked
3. Refrigerate in a clean, sealed container
4. Expect to need 3-4 feedings to revive fully

### Backup Preservation
1. Spread a thin layer of active starter on parchment
2. Allow to dry completely at room temperature
3. Break into flakes and store in airtight container
4. To revive, soak flakes in warm water before feeding

## Preparing for Baking

1. Take refrigerated starter out 24 hours before baking
2. Feed twice at room temperature before using
3. Use starter when it has doubled and shows good activity
4. For best results, use starter at its peak or just starting to recede

## Maintaining a Baking Schedule

### Weekend Baking Routine
- **Monday-Thursday**: Keep in refrigerator
- **Friday morning**: Remove from refrigerator, feed
- **Friday evening**: Feed again
- **Saturday morning**: Starter ready for baking
- **Saturday evening**: Feed for maintenance or return to refrigerator

### Daily Baking Routine
- Maintain a smaller amount at room temperature
- Feed daily at consistent times
- Scale up when needed for specific recipes
- Consider keeping a backup portion refrigerated
